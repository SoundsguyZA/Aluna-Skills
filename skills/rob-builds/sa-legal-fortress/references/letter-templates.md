# SA Letter Templates

Tested forms for common SA legal correspondence in shareholder, labour, and contract matters. Adapt the bracketed sections to the specific matter. Do not use templates blind; every letter must be calibrated to the strategic posture and the specific facts.

## Letter of Demand (General)

```
OPEN

[Date]

[Recipient name]
[Recipient address]

By email: [email] and by hand / registered post

Dear [Salutation],

LETTER OF DEMAND: [Brief subject]

1. I refer to [the relevant background documents or correspondence] (the Background).

2. The relevant facts are as follows:

   2.1 [Fact one with date and reference to document where applicable]

   2.2 [Fact two]

   2.3 [Fact three]

3. The conduct set out above constitutes:

   3.1 a breach of [section of statute] in that [specific element]; and

   3.2 a breach of [contractual provision] in that [specific element].

4. I therefore demand that you, within [number] ([number in words]) days of the date of this letter:

   4.1 [Specific action one];

   4.2 [Specific action two]; and

   4.3 [Specific action three].

5. Failing your compliance with the demand set out in paragraph 4 above within the period stated, I will, without further notice to you, institute proceedings in the High Court of South Africa, [Division], for the following relief:

   5.1 [Specific relief sought];

   5.2 An order that you pay the costs of suit on the attorney and own client scale; and

   5.3 Such further or alternative relief as the Honourable Court deems just.

6. I reserve all my rights, including but not limited to my rights under [relevant statute] and the common law.

Yours faithfully,

___________________________
ROB BARENBRUG
[Capacity: Shareholder / Director / Contracting Party]
[Contact details]
```

## Section 26 Demand for Company Records

```
OPEN

[Date]

The Directors
Rib Rage [full registered name] (Pty) Ltd
[Registered address]

By email: [email] and by hand / registered post

Dear Sirs / Mesdames,

DEMAND FOR ACCESS TO COMPANY RECORDS IN TERMS OF SECTION 26 OF THE COMPANIES ACT 71 OF 2008

1. I am the registered holder of [number] ordinary shares in Rib Rage [full name] (Pty) Ltd (the Company), being [percentage]% of the issued share capital of the Company.

2. In my capacity as shareholder, and in terms of section 26 of the Companies Act 71 of 2008, I have a right of access to the records of the Company.

3. I hereby demand, in terms of section 26, access to and copies of the following records:

   3.1 The Memorandum of Incorporation of the Company and any amendments thereto;

   3.2 Any rules of the Company;

   3.3 The securities register of the Company;

   3.4 The minutes of all meetings of shareholders of the Company for the past three (3) years;

   3.5 All resolutions adopted by the shareholders of the Company for the past three (3) years;

   3.6 The annual financial statements of the Company for the past three (3) years;

   3.7 Notices and minutes of all meetings of directors and directors' committees, and any written resolutions of directors, for the past three (3) years; and

   3.8 The accounting records of the Company sufficient to enable me to ascertain the financial position of the Company.

4. I require the documents identified above to be made available to me within fourteen (14) days of the date of this letter, at the registered office of the Company during business hours, or alternatively delivered to me in electronic format.

5. I record that any refusal of or failure to comply with this demand constitutes an offence in terms of section 26(9) of the Act, and entitles me to apply for relief in terms of section 26(7) of the Act and the Promotion of Access to Information Act 2 of 2000.

6. I reserve all my rights.

Yours faithfully,

___________________________
ROB BARENBRUG
Shareholder
[Contact details]
```

## Reply to Attorney Letter

```
OPEN

[Date]

[Attorney firm name]
[Address]

By email: [email]

Your ref: [reference]
Our ref: [our reference if applicable]

Dear Sirs / Mesdames,

YOUR LETTER DATED [DATE]: [SUBJECT]

1. I acknowledge receipt of your letter dated [date], the contents of which have been carefully noted.

2. The factual position is set out below. Where your letter has misstated the facts, I record the correct position. Where your letter has omitted material facts, I supply them. Silence on any matter set out in your letter is not to be construed as acceptance of any statement in your letter.

3. As regards the factual position:

   3.1 [Correct fact one with reference to documentary support]

   3.2 [Correct fact two]

   3.3 [Material fact omitted by your client, now placed on the record]

4. As regards the legal position:

   4.1 [Statement of correct legal position with reference to the relevant statute and section]

   4.2 [Identification of any legal error in the attorney's letter]

5. Your client's conduct, as set out in paragraphs [X] to [Y] above, constitutes [breach / unfairly prejudicial conduct / unlawful act] for which I reserve all rights and remedies.

6. I do not accept your client's [position / characterisation / demand] for the reasons set out above.

7. To the extent that your client wishes to engage with the matters set out herein on a without prejudice basis with a view to settlement, I am open to such engagement. In the absence of such engagement within fourteen (14) days of the date of this letter, I will proceed as advised.

8. I reserve all my rights.

Yours faithfully,

___________________________
ROB BARENBRUG
[Capacity]
[Contact details]
```

## Without Prejudice Settlement Offer

```
WITHOUT PREJUDICE SAVE AS TO COSTS

[Date]

[Recipient]
[Address]

By email: [email]

Dear [Salutation],

WITHOUT PREJUDICE OFFER OF SETTLEMENT: [SUBJECT]

1. This letter is written without prejudice save as to costs and is made bona fide with a view to the settlement of the dispute between us.

2. Without admitting any of the factual or legal positions taken by you or by me in this matter, and solely for the purpose of avoiding the further expenditure of time and cost, I propose the following terms of settlement:

   2.1 [Term one]

   2.2 [Term two]

   2.3 [Term three]

   2.4 Each party to bear their own costs to date.

   2.5 The settlement is in full and final settlement of all claims of every nature whatsoever arising out of or in connection with [the relevant subject matter].

   2.6 The terms of the settlement and the negotiations leading to it are to remain strictly confidential between the parties.

3. This offer is open for acceptance for a period of fourteen (14) days from the date of this letter, after which it lapses and is withdrawn.

4. If this offer is not accepted, I reserve the right to bring the existence and terms of this offer to the attention of the court on the question of costs after judgment, in accordance with the Calderbank principle.

5. I look forward to your response.

Yours faithfully,

___________________________
ROB BARENBRUG
[Capacity]
[Contact details]
```

## Notice of Constructive Dismissal Referral

```
OPEN

[Date]

[Employer name]
[Employer address]

By email and by hand

Dear Sirs / Mesdames,

NOTICE OF RESIGNATION AND REFERRAL OF CONSTRUCTIVE DISMISSAL DISPUTE

1. I refer to the events set out below, which collectively constitute conduct on the part of [Employer] that has made my continued employment intolerable.

2. The relevant events are:

   2.1 [Event one with date]

   2.2 [Event two with date]

   2.3 [Event three with date]

3. I have made repeated attempts to address these matters, including [list attempts], without resolution.

4. I have no reasonable alternative to terminating my employment in these circumstances.

5. I therefore tender my resignation with immediate effect.

6. I record that my resignation is a constructive dismissal within the meaning of section 186(1)(e) of the Labour Relations Act 66 of 1995. I will be referring an unfair dismissal dispute to the Commission for Conciliation, Mediation and Arbitration within the thirty (30) day period prescribed by section 191 of the Act.

7. I reserve all my rights including but not limited to my rights under the Labour Relations Act, the Basic Conditions of Employment Act, and the common law.

Yours faithfully,

___________________________
ROB BARENBRUG
[Position]
```

## Calibration Notes

These templates are starting points, not finished products. Before sending any letter:

Confirm every factual statement against documentary evidence. A letter that misstates a fact undermines its own credibility and provides ammunition for the other side.

Calibrate the tone to the strategic posture. A first letter is typically more measured than a follow up after non-compliance.

Confirm the prescription period. Many SA legal remedies have hard time limits. Constructive dismissal: 30 days from resignation. Unfair labour practice: 90 days. EEA: 6 months. Companies Act applications are subject to delay defences but no fixed prescription.

Choose the right recipient. Letters to the company go to the directors at the registered office. Letters to an individual go to that individual. Letters to an attorney go to the attorney with the client's name in the subject.

Choose the right delivery method. Email creates a record but may be denied as not received. Registered post creates a delivery receipt. Hand delivery creates the strongest record but requires logistics. For important letters, combine email with registered post or sheriff delivery.

Keep a complete file. Every letter sent must be saved with delivery proof, in a folder organised by date. The file becomes the evidence record for any subsequent proceedings.
