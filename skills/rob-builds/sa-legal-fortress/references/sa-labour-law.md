# SA Labour Law Reference

The Labour Relations Act 66 of 1995 (LRA), Basic Conditions of Employment Act 75 of 1997 (BCEA), and Employment Equity Act 55 of 1998 (EEA) form the spine of SA labour law. Most disputes go through the CCMA before the Labour Court.

## When Labour Law Applies

The LRA applies to employees, defined in section 213 as anyone who works for another person and receives or is entitled to receive remuneration, or any other person who in any manner assists in carrying on or conducting the business of an employer. The Code of Good Practice on Who is an Employee and the presumption in section 200A are decisive in disguised employment cases.

Independent contractors are excluded. But the courts look at substance over form. A person labelled a "consultant" but treated as an employee (controlled hours, exclusive service, integration into business, economic dependence) will be found to be an employee. This matters for Rob if any Rib Rage arrangement is dressed up as something it is not.

Directors and shareholders can simultaneously be employees, if they perform work and receive remuneration in that capacity. The fact of directorship does not exclude employee status.

## Dismissals (LRA Chapter VIII)

**Section 185: Right not to be unfairly dismissed.** Every employee has the right not to be unfairly dismissed.

**Section 186: Meaning of dismissal.** Dismissal includes:
- Termination by the employer with or without notice
- Failure to renew a fixed term contract where the employee reasonably expected renewal on the same or similar terms
- Refusal to allow an employee to resume work after maternity leave
- Selective re-employment after dismissal of a group
- Resignation made or rendered intolerable by employer conduct (constructive dismissal)
- Refusal to continue employment after a transfer under s197

Constructive dismissal under s186(1)(e) is the relevant route where an employer makes continued employment intolerable. The employee must prove:
- Resignation
- The employer made continued employment intolerable
- The intolerability was caused by the employer
- There was no reasonable alternative to resignation

**Section 188: Fair reason and fair procedure.** A dismissal is unfair if the employer fails to prove that the reason was fair (related to conduct, capacity, or operational requirements) and that the dismissal was effected in accordance with a fair procedure.

**Section 191: Dispute resolution.**
- Unfair dismissal disputes must be referred to the CCMA (or accredited bargaining council) within 30 days of the dismissal
- Unfair labour practice disputes within 90 days
- Conciliation is compulsory
- If conciliation fails, the matter proceeds to arbitration (for misconduct and incapacity dismissals) or to the Labour Court (for operational requirements, automatically unfair, and discrimination dismissals)

**Remedies for unfair dismissal:**
- Reinstatement (the primary remedy under s193)
- Re-employment
- Compensation up to 12 months remuneration for ordinary unfair dismissal
- Compensation up to 24 months for automatically unfair dismissals (those listed in s187, including dismissals related to pregnancy, union activity, whistleblowing, refusal to do unlawful work)

## Unfair Labour Practices (Section 186(2))

Defined as any unfair act or omission between employer and employee involving:
- Unfair conduct relating to promotion, demotion, probation, training, or benefits
- Unfair suspension or disciplinary action short of dismissal
- Failure to reinstate or re-employ in terms of an agreement
- Occupational detriment in breach of the Protected Disclosures Act

90 day referral period to the CCMA.

## BCEA Essentials

**Notice (section 37):**
- Less than 6 months service: 1 week
- 6 months to 1 year: 2 weeks
- 1 year or more: 4 weeks

Notice must be in writing except where the employee is illiterate. Notice cannot run concurrently with annual leave.

**Severance pay (section 41):** On dismissal for operational requirements, the employee is entitled to at least one week's remuneration per completed year of service. This is the statutory minimum; contractual or bargaining council provisions may be more generous.

**Working time, leave, and overtime:** sections 9 to 22. Standard working week 45 hours, overtime by agreement, overtime pay at 1.5x normal wage. Annual leave 21 consecutive days. Sick leave 30 days over 36 month cycle. Family responsibility leave 3 days per year.

## Restraints of Trade

Governed by common law, not statute. A restraint is enforceable unless it is unreasonable. The Magna Alloys test (Magna Alloys and Research SA v Ellis 1984 4 SA 874 (A)) places the onus on the party resisting enforcement to prove unreasonableness.

Factors considered:
- Is there a protectable interest (confidential information, trade connections, customer base)?
- Is the protectable interest threatened?
- Is the restraint reasonable in scope (geographic, temporal, activity)?
- Is the restraint contrary to public policy?

Restraints in the employment context are scrutinised more carefully than restraints in the sale of a business context, where they are generally upheld more readily.

## Discrimination (EEA)

The Employment Equity Act prohibits unfair discrimination on listed grounds (race, gender, sex, pregnancy, marital status, family responsibility, ethnic or social origin, colour, sexual orientation, age, disability, religion, HIV status, conscience, belief, political opinion, culture, language, birth) and on any other arbitrary ground.

Claims go to the Labour Court via section 10 of the EEA. The 2014 amendments allow disputes about unfair discrimination in remuneration (equal pay for equal work) to be referred to the CCMA.

## Practical CCMA Notes

CCMA referral is by completing form 7.11. The form is short and asks for the nature of the dispute, the parties, the date of dismissal or the unfair act, the remedy sought, and an outline of the facts.

Conciliation is informal. The commissioner attempts to facilitate settlement. If no settlement is reached, a certificate of non-resolution is issued and the matter proceeds.

Arbitration is more formal. Evidence is led, witnesses are called, documents are admitted. The commissioner's award is final and binding, subject only to review by the Labour Court on the narrow grounds in section 145 of the LRA.

Legal representation at CCMA arbitration is restricted in misconduct and incapacity disputes. The commissioner has a discretion to allow legal representation. Otherwise the parties are represented by themselves, a co-employee, or a trade union or employers' organisation official.

## Application to Rib Rage Context

If Rob has at any point been an employee of Rib Rage (whether titled as director, manager, executive, or under any other label) and has been removed or constructively dismissed, the labour law remedies sit in parallel to the Companies Act remedies. The two streams must be coordinated, not run in isolation.

Constructive dismissal under s186(1)(e) is the relevant doctrine if Rob was made the subject of intolerable conduct designed to push him out of his employment role while leaving his shareholding theoretically intact. This includes exclusion from operational decisions he was previously entitled to participate in, withdrawal of resources or authority, public undermining, or removal of remuneration without consent.

The CCMA route has a 30 day prescription period from the date of dismissal. If Rob is contemplating constructive dismissal, the clock starts on the date he resigns or is treated as resigned. This is a hard deadline. A s163 application has no equivalent strict deadline but is subject to delay defences.

The interaction between a s163 buyout claim and a constructive dismissal compensation claim is fact-specific. Compensation under the LRA is capped at 12 months remuneration. Buyout under s163 reflects fair value of the shares. They are different forms of relief and can co-exist.
