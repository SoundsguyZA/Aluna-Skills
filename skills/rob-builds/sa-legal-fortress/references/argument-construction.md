# Argument Construction Methodology

How to build a watertight legal argument under SA law: one that cannot be flanked, undermined, or used against Rob. This applies to every outgoing communication, every draft pleading, every memo, and every position taken in correspondence.

## The IRAC Spine

Every legal argument under SA law has the same skeleton:

**Issue.** What is the legal question to be answered. State it narrowly. "Whether Mike Barenbrug's conduct in excluding Rob from Rib Rage board meetings since June 2024 constitutes unfairly prejudicial conduct under section 163 of the Companies Act 71 of 2008." Not "whether things are unfair".

**Rule.** What is the applicable law. Cite the statute and section. Cite the case if you know it is real and relevant. Quote the text of the section if it is the load-bearing element of the argument. Do not summarise where the actual text is short enough to quote.

**Application.** Apply the rule to the facts. This is where most arguments fail. Each element of the rule must be addressed against each material fact. If section 163 requires conduct that "unfairly disregards the interests" of the applicant, then each of "conduct", "unfairly", "disregards", and "interests of the applicant" must be addressed with reference to the specific factual record.

**Conclusion.** State the result the law produces. State the relief sought. Do not hedge.

## Element by Element Application

The dominant analytical move in SA legal writing is element-by-element application. Break the legal rule into its constituent requirements and address each one with the supporting fact and document reference.

For example, applying s163 to Rob's matter:

| Element | Facts | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Applicant is a shareholder | Rob holds [X]% in Rib Rage | CIPC CoR 39, share register |
| Conduct of company or director | Mike refused to convene 2025 AGM | WhatsApp 14 March 2025 |
| Result is oppressive or prejudicial | Rob excluded from decisions on dividend policy | Board minutes 1 Aug 2025 (Rob not invited) |
| Unfairly disregards Rob's interests | Mike paid himself R85k/month while refusing Rob's R35k consulting fee | Bank statements, consulting proposal, email response |

Every element must be checked. Where an element is weak, name it as weak and propose the additional evidence needed.

## Evidence Hierarchy

Not all evidence is equal. Build arguments on the strongest available evidence.

**Primary evidence (strongest):**
- Original signed contracts and resolutions
- CIPC filings and statutory records
- Bank statements
- Audited financial statements
- Court orders and statutory documents

**Secondary evidence:**
- Unsigned drafts
- Email correspondence between principals
- WhatsApp messages between principals
- Minutes (where signed) of meetings actually held

**Tertiary evidence (weakest):**
- Hearsay accounts from third parties
- Recollections without contemporaneous documents
- Verbal undertakings without confirmation
- Industry custom or general practice arguments

When constructing an argument, lead with primary evidence wherever available. Where only secondary or tertiary evidence supports a point, flag the weakness explicitly so it can be addressed before the document goes out.

## Exhibit Referencing

In SA practice, supporting documents are referenced as annexures or exhibits. The convention in affidavits is "Annexure A", "Annexure B" etc, with each annexure attached and paginated continuously with the affidavit.

In correspondence, refer to enclosed documents by clear reference: "I attach the consulting proposal dated 12 February 2026 (the Consulting Proposal) for ease of reference."

When the document referenced is the writer's own prior correspondence, use a date and a short descriptor: "As set out in our letter dated 3 April 2026..."

When referring to a section of a statute, cite as: "section 163 of the Companies Act 71 of 2008" on first reference. Thereafter "section 163" or "s163" is acceptable.

When referring to a case, cite as: "Visser Sitrus (Pty) Ltd v Goede Hoop Sitrus (Pty) Ltd 2014 (5) SA 179 (WCC)". Only cite cases you know to be real. If uncertain, cite the principle without the case name.

## Without Prejudice vs Open Correspondence

Decide deliberately for each piece of correspondence whether it is on the open record or without prejudice. The choice has strategic consequences.

**Open correspondence:**
- Letters of demand
- Statements of position
- Notices required by statute or contract
- Anything that should be available to a court
- Anything that creates an evidentiary record Rob wants to rely on

**Without prejudice correspondence:**
- Genuine settlement proposals
- Concessions made in pursuit of resolution
- Negotiation positions Rob does not want to be tied to later

Mark each letter with its status on the first line. "OPEN" or "WITHOUT PREJUDICE". If a letter is partly open and partly settlement focused, split it into two letters.

The Rib Rage consulting proposal is open correspondence by design. It functions as evidence of Rob's reasonable conduct in any future s163 application.

## Letter of Demand Requirements

A letter of demand in SA practice typically includes:

**Identification of the parties.** "I act for myself in the matter set out below, and address this letter to you in your capacity as [director / shareholder / co-shareholder]."

**Statement of the relevant facts.** Set out the facts chronologically. Do not editorialise. Do not insult. State what happened, when, with reference to documents where they exist.

**Identification of the legal position.** State the breach, the statutory or contractual basis, and the consequence. "This conduct constitutes a breach of section 76(3)(b) of the Companies Act 71 of 2008 in that..."

**Demand.** State precisely what is demanded. The demand must be capable of being complied with. "I demand that you, within 14 (fourteen) days of the date of this letter, [specific action]."

**Consequence of non-compliance.** State what will follow non-compliance. The threat must be credible and specific. "Failing your compliance within the period stated, I will, without further notice to you, institute proceedings in the High Court of South Africa, KwaZulu-Natal Local Division, Durban, for the relief set out herein, and you will be held liable for all costs of suit on the attorney and own client scale."

**Reservation of rights.** Standard closing. "I reserve all rights, including but not limited to my rights under the Companies Act 71 of 2008, the common law, and any applicable shareholders agreement."

**Signature and date.** Original signature where possible. Scanned copy for email transmission.

## Counter Argument Stress Test

Before any document goes out, run the stress test:

What is the strongest argument the other side can make in response? Write it out in their voice. If the strongest response defeats Rob's position, the position needs to be redrafted before sending. If the strongest response only partially defeats Rob's position, identify which paragraph carries the weakness and either strengthen it or anticipate and pre-empt the response in the document.

Who is the audience? A letter to Mike will be read by Mike, by Mike's attorney, and potentially by a court. Each reader is looking for different things. The letter must work for all of them simultaneously.

What does the document admit? Every assertion is also a concession. If Rob's letter says "I have been excluded from board meetings since June 2024", he has admitted he was not at meetings before June 2024, which may or may not be advantageous. Check every statement for what it concedes.

What does the document leave open? Anything not addressed is preserved for later. Anything addressed is potentially closed off. Choose deliberately which battles to fight in any given letter.

## Tone

Tone is a tactical choice, not a personality choice.

For correspondence intended to establish a record of reasonableness (such as the consulting proposal), the tone is calm, factual, and constructive. No threats. No emotion. Pure offer and reasonable framing.

For correspondence intended to create pressure (such as a letter of demand), the tone is formal, precise, and firm. No insults. No moralising. Clear statement of legal position and consequence.

For correspondence in response to provocation, the tone is unaffected by the provocation. Respond to the legal substance only. Ignore the personal. Where the other side has been intemperate, the contrast with Rob's measured tone becomes part of the record.

For internal strategic notes (Rob's eyes only), the tone is whatever Rob finds useful. Direct, honest, including the colourful assessment of the other side's tactics. None of it goes out.

## Output Standards for Drafted Letters

Every drafted letter must:

Use SA English spelling consistently.

Avoid em dashes. Use commas or full stops.

Use formal salutations (Dear Sir, Dear Mr Barenbrug) appropriate to the relationship and the formality of the matter.

Include a clear reference line (Our ref: / Your ref:) where applicable.

Be dated.

Identify the document type at the head (Letter of Demand / Reply / Notice).

State the status (OPEN / WITHOUT PREJUDICE).

Use numbered paragraphs for letters of any substance.

Include a clear demand or position in the final substantive paragraph.

End with a formal closing (Yours faithfully) and Rob's name, capacity, and contact details.

Be saved as a properly formatted document, not a chat message.
