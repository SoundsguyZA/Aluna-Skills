## Strategic Posture

Rob's current strategy has the following layers:

**Layer 1: Open offer for resolution.** The consulting proposal is a calibrated offer that gives Mike a face-saving way to recognise Rob's continued contribution and preserve a working family relationship. It is reasonable on its terms (R35k/month is modest in the context of a profitable FMCG business; the 5-year evergreen term creates stability for both sides).

**Layer 2: Evidentiary positioning.** The consulting proposal is on the open record. Its function is dual: it offers resolution, and it creates evidence of Rob's reasonable conduct. Whether accepted, rejected, or ignored, it strengthens Rob's position in any subsequent application.

**Layer 3: Litigation roadmap.** If the proposal is dismissed or ignored, the next step is to instruct an attorney and prepare a section 163 application under the Companies Act for relief from oppressive or unfairly prejudicial conduct. The proposal letter becomes Annexure A to the founding affidavit.

**Layer 4: Family relationship management.** The proposal is sent through email (formal record) and WhatsApp (informal acknowledgement). The choice of channels reflects the dual nature of the relationship. The 7-day window is firm but not aggressive. The tone allows Mike to engage without losing face.

## What to Watch For (Incoming Communications)

Incoming communications in this matter are likely to use the following tactics. Apply the communication scrutiny methodology with these specific markers in mind:

**Family framing of legal issues.** "We are a family. This should not be a legal matter." This framing is designed to dissuade Rob from asserting his legal rights. The response: this is both a family matter and a legal matter, and the legal rights exist whether or not the family relationship is invoked.

**Minimisation of Rob's contribution.** "Rob has not been involved in the day to day for some time." This is designed to set up an argument that Rob's shareholder rights have somehow been diluted by absence. They have not. Shareholder rights do not depend on operational involvement.

**Suggestion of unanimity.** "The family / the board / the other shareholders are all in agreement." Test this by asking for written confirmation from each party allegedly in agreement.

**Reasonableness theatre.** "We are happy to discuss but cannot accept your demands." Where Rob's demands are reasonable (and the consulting proposal is reasonable), this is a position of refusal dressed up as reasonableness. Treat as rejection.

**Procedural delay.** "We need time to consider this with the other shareholders and our advisors." Acceptable for a short period (7 to 14 days). After that it becomes delay.

**Counter-allegations.** "Rob has himself acted in breach of his duties." If Rob has not so acted, this is fabricated leverage. Address head on with documentary refutation. If there is any factual basis to a counter-allegation, address it specifically and separate it from the main grievance.

**Without prejudice misuse.** Letters marked "without prejudice" that contain demands, threats, or admissions that are not genuinely part of settlement negotiation. The privilege does not apply to such content.

## What to Avoid (Outgoing Communications)

In any correspondence Rob sends in this matter:

Never accept characterisations of his conduct that are inaccurate. Silence on a characterisation is taken as acceptance.

Never agree to procedural arrangements that delay matters without a clear deadline.

Never accept partial offers without expressly reserving the balance of claims.

Never speak in absolutes about future conduct ("I will never...") because positions may need to evolve.

Never engage with personal or emotional content. Respond to the legal substance only.

Never copy other family members into legal correspondence unless they have a defined legal role in the matter. Copying creates witnesses who may be examined later.

Never use email signatures that downplay Rob's status. Sign in capacity as shareholder where shareholder rights are being asserted.

Never accept "settlement" that does not include a full and final settlement clause and a confidentiality clause, where appropriate.
