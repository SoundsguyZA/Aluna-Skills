# Commercial Outcomes

Before substantial proposal work, establish the customer objective, decision constraints, essential versus optional scope, approximate budget if appropriate, decision timing and competing options where known. Ask only missing questions material to the work. Offer alternatives or phased scopes without misrepresenting suitability or sacrificing required safety.

After feedback, record date, anonymous job reference or authorised private record ID, work done, time spent/saved if known, offer, outcome, customer's exact stated reason (mark paraphrase), supporting evidence, unknowns and next step. Never infer the true reason from the objection alone. Do not publish customer identities, addresses, financial details or private records in shared repositories.

Look for patterns across comparable cases, using clear denominators and sample sizes. A single rejection may warrant a qualification experiment, not a blanket pricing policy. Test changes against conversion, time, margin and customer suitability. Obtain approval for pricing, communications and commitments.

## Acceptance scenarios
- Customer says too expensive: log the stated objection; budget and competing quote remain unknown.
- Offer premium and staged options only if technically suitable and commercially approved.
- No price list: prepare a technical proposal with pricing pending rather than inventing amounts.
